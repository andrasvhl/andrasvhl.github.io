Improve Product

A design council that tests its own work.

Six specialists design a change to your product from how people actually used it, show you mockups before anything is built, then use the real thing in a sandbox until nothing serious is left.

01

The idea

Most product changes are designed from opinions and checked by looking at them. This skill turns that around. It starts from what people really did, lets six specialists disagree on purpose, shows you the result before a line is built, and then has every one of them use the finished thing, hands on, until it holds up.

03

Four principles

Stories come from real use

It reads your logs, chats and task history for what people were trying to get done and what it cost them. The council designs for the goal, not a replay of the old clicks. Stories live in a private library that each run refreshes as your product changes, recent and complex ones first.

Testing happens in a real sandbox

A separate copy of your product with realistic data, where every role clicks, types, sends and submits. Nothing leaves it. The first run walks you through setting one up, wherever your product runs.

Design, then test, then again

Two design rounds end in one proposal you approve. Then the roles use the build, one at a time on fresh data, fix what they find and test again until nothing serious is left.

Delight comes last

Once it works, is reliable and is easy to use, the product manager picks one or two moments where it should beat your expectation: it already knew, it took fewer steps, it ended well. Competence, not confetti.

The council

Owns integrity

Systems designer

Concept design: one purpose per concept, a state table where every action has an exact undo, and rules the data must always keep.

1 vote
Owns value

Product manager

The job in one sentence, the announcement written before the design, a list of what not to build. Decides on delight.

1 vote
Owns usability and beauty

UI designer

Apple’s design principles and Dieter Rams. Renders the mockups you react to, every state, with restraint, rhythm and finish.

1 vote
Owns understanding

Copywriter

Purpose, anticipation, context, empathy. One word per concept, buttons that say what will happen, errors that say what to do.

1 vote
Owns reliability

QA engineer

Obsessively meticulous. Varies structure, data, time and platform, compares against every oracle, and trusts nothing until reproduced.

0.75 vote · Full
Owns behaviour

Psychologist

Finds what really blocks people (capability, opportunity or motivation), designs the peak and the end, and vetoes dark patterns.

0.75 vote · Full

Small Council runs the first four; Full Council adds the last two at a slightly lower vote. A lower vote never outvotes evidence: a reproduced bug counts as a finding. Each role is briefed with a method from its field, because a title like “world class” alone barely changes what a model produces.

05

How a run goes

  1. StoriesPicks 5 to 10 stories from the library for the page or flow you are improving, then adds new ones from fresh data.
  2. AloneEach role writes its own thoughts without seeing the others. The UI designer drafts two or three mockup directions.
  3. TogetherThey discuss each other’s thoughts and the mockups, and vote on the named conflicts.
  4. YouYou see the mockups, what would be built and every change to today’s behaviour, numbered. Approve, or react in your own words; it revises until you approve.You decide
  5. BuildTests first, for every behaviour that is meant to change.
  6. TestOne role at a time, each on a fresh sandbox, pursues the goal of every story and reports what beat expectation and what fell short.
  7. AgainFix, test again, until no serious finding is left or three rounds have passed.↺ back to Test
  8. ShipA last systems check, then your go. Results go back into the story library for next time.You decide
